# Claude Code for the Curious

**What happens when you tell your computer what to build and it actually does it.**

Talk given at [Women in Product Nordics](https://www.linkedin.com/company/women-in-product-nordics),
15 April 2026, Matrikel1, Copenhagen.

**[Open the deck](https://olgasafonova.github.io/claude-code-for-the-curious/)**

## What it covers

Written for product managers and designers rather than engineers. No prior coding
required, and nothing in it assumes you want to become a developer.

- What Claude Code actually is: you describe the outcome, it works out the steps
- Things worth asking it that have nothing to do with writing code, like comparing
  competitor pages, finding every mention of pricing in your docs, or turning a
  requirements doc into user stories
- Two workflows from my own week: building a daily standup from meeting notes and
  issue trackers, and researching a colleague's question across source code, wiki
  and help articles
- What I learned the hard way, and where it still goes wrong

The live portion took requests from the room: explain a public repo, visualise the
evening's own attendee list, or add a slide to this deck while it was on screen.

## Files

- `index.html` is the deck. 11 slides, single file, no build step. Open it in a browser.
- `icons/` holds the QR codes and platform logos for the closing slide.

Arrow keys, space, or page up and down move between slides. Home and End jump to the
ends. Press F for full screen.

## What is not in this repo

Two things shown on the night are deliberately absent, and the deck says so on the
slides where they sat.

- **The two screen recordings.** Both were captured on a work machine. They show
  internal repository paths and colleagues by name, so they are not published. The
  slides carry a short note in their place.
- **The attendee visualisation.** It was built live from the evening's registration
  list. Publishing roles and employers of people who signed up for a community meetup
  is not mine to do, so those files stay private.

Neither omission changes the argument, and leaving them out is the same discipline the
talk is about.
